02

Abstract

This portfolio presents an autonomous maze-navigation system for a simulated TurtleBot3 Burger. The robot starts at cell A5 of a procedurally generated 5x5 maze and must reach cell E1 without a global map or predefined path planner. A new maze layout is generated every episode using randomised recursive backtracking, so the policy must learn general navigation rather than a fixed route. All training and evaluation used a PyBullet physics simulation.

The agent observes a 52-dimensional vector combining 24 LiDAR proximity readings, 3 CNN-based goal-detection outputs, and 25 visited-cell binary flags (full breakdown in section 4.1). It acts via continuous wheel velocity commands. PPO from Stable-Baselines3 trains across 10 parallel environments for approximately one million timesteps, guided by a shaped reward function detailed in section 6.

Training was iterative. An early policy at around 600,000 timesteps showed partial success in raw metrics but was prone to getting trapped in repeated paths due to insufficient spatial context in the observation. Adding visited-cell tracking resolved this failure mode. Final results and analysis are in sections 7 and 8.

System Overview

Four processing stages run every simulation step. CNN inference is rate-limited to every tenth step and only within 10 m of the goal, sustaining approximately 300 steps per second across 10 parallel training environments.

Stage 1 ⟟

LiDAR Sweep

24 rays over a 360° arc, 3 m range. Normalised hit fractions form the first 24 observation elements. Details in section 5.

pybullet · rayTestBatch · 3 m
Stage 2 ◉

Goal Detection

Colour-based beacon detector returning a detection flag, confidence score, and horizontal offset. Details in section 5.

numpy · PIL · colour filter
Stage 3 ◈

Observation Assembly

LiDAR, vision outputs, and visited-cell flags concatenated into a 52-float array. Full index breakdown in section 4.1.

gymnasium · Box(52,) · float32
Stage 4 ⊞

PPO Policy

MlpPolicy maps the 52-dim observation to continuous wheel velocities. Full configuration in section 6.1.

stable-baselines3 · CUDA · MlpPolicy

4.1 Observation Vector: 52 Dimensions

Index Dim. Signal Range Description
[0 : 24] 24 LiDAR hit-fractions [0, 1] Normalised ray-cast distances. 0 indicates near-contact; 1 indicates open space at the 3 m maximum range. Rays are uniformly spaced over 360° at chassis height (0.15 m).
[24] 1 CNN detection flag {0, 1} 1 if the green goal beacon is visible in the current camera frame and the matching pixel count exceeds the minimum threshold.
[25] 1 CNN confidence [0, 1] Proportion of matching green pixels in the downsampled frame, capped at 1.0. Provides a graded signal proportional to how much of the goal is visible.
[26] 1 CNN horizontal offset [-1, +1] Bounding-box centre relative to the image centre, normalised to the half image width. Negative when the goal is left of centre; positive when right.
[27 : 52] 25 Visited-cell flags {0, 1} Binary episode memory: 1 if the cell has been visited during this run, 0 if not. Resets to all zeros at the start of each episode.
Total 52 Concatenated float32 array passed directly to MlpPolicy as a flat input vector.

4.2 Simulation Environment

Maze Generation

Recursive Backtracking DFS, 5x5 Grid

Each episode generates a new layout using a randomised depth-first search with backtracking (maze_grid.py), guaranteeing exactly one valid solution path. Wall segments overlap slightly at shared junctions, eliminating the passable gaps that earlier fixed-length geometry produced. Randomising the layout each episode forces the policy to learn general corridor navigation rather than a fixed route.

Physics Simulation

PyBullet Rigid-Body Scene

The scene includes a textured ground plane, per-episode hedge walls, a goal cabin, and a tall green beacon cylinder. The beacon has no collision shape, so LiDAR rays pass through it and the robot is never penalised for approaching its own goal. Wall contacts are detected via getContactPoints with a secondary LiDAR threshold (hit-fraction < 0.01) as a fail-safe. Wheel velocities are set via VELOCITY_CONTROL with a turn-rate limiter to prevent abrupt spins.

4.3 Codebase

turtlebot3_maze_env.py

Core Gymnasium environment. Manages the PyBullet physics lifecycle, LiDAR raycasting, CNN inference scheduling, observation assembly, visited-cell tracking, and reward computation.

maze_grid.py

DFS maze generator (see section 4.2). Exports shared constants START_POS, GOAL_POS, and CELL_SIZE used by the environment and GUI.

goal_detector.py

CNN-inspired colour detector described in section 5. Returns a detection flag, confidence score, and horizontal directional offset to the environment.

train.py

Training entry point. Centralises all reward hyperparameters in a single config dictionary, vectorises 10 parallel environments, initialises WandB logging, and supports incremental training from a saved checkpoint.

train_metrics.py

Custom SB3 callback. Logs success rate, collision rate, mean episode reward, and mean steps-to-goal to WandB and TensorBoard every 10 completed episodes.

navigator_gui.py

Tkinter dashboard with splash screen, home menu, live top-down maze render, camera feed with CNN bounding-box overlay, LiDAR ring visualisation, and visited-cell heatmap.

05

Perception and CNN Goal Detection

Two sensor modalities feed the observation vector: LiDAR for proximity awareness, and a colour-based goal detector for directional guidance when the beacon is in view.

Range Sensing

24-Ray LiDAR, Full 360° Arc

Twenty-four rays are fired uniformly at chassis height (0.15 m) using PyBullet's rayTestBatch, each with a 3 m range. Hit fractions normalised to [0, 1] give the policy local proximity information without constructing an explicit map. Front-sector rays (indices 0 to 2 and 21 to 23) also feed the reward computation; their role in gating the CNN centering bonus is described in section 6.2.

Computer Vision

CNN-Inspired Colour-Based Goal Detector

The forward camera renders a 160x120 RGB frame. The pipeline in goal_detector.py performs channel extraction, 4x max-pooling to reduce noise, and threshold classification (R<100, G>150, B<100) to isolate the green beacon. A bounding box is fitted to the matched region; its horizontal centre normalised to [-1, +1] gives the directional offset. Detection is cached every 10 steps and only runs within 10 m of the goal, maintaining approximately 300 steps per second during training.

PPO was selected for its stability with continuous action spaces and dense reward signals. Each reward component was added to address a specific observed failure mode; the justification for each is noted below.

Rollout-buffer overshoot: With 10 environments and n_steps = 1,024, each rollout collects 10,240 steps before an update. The final run therefore logged approximately 1,003,520 total timesteps, a small overshoot of the 1,000,000 target reflecting normal buffer-alignment behaviour.

6.2 Reward Design

Values are taken directly from train.py. Positive terms guide goal-seeking and exploration; negative terms penalise collisions, inefficiency, and unsafe steering.

+
Goal Reached

Large terminal bonus plus an efficiency multiplier based on steps remaining. Faster solutions earn significantly more than slow ones.

+2,000 + 2,000 x (steps_remaining / 6,000)
+
Distance Progress

Internal shaping signal computed by the environment, not observed by the agent. Rewards net progress toward the goal each step without exposing the distance to the robot.

+20 x delta_dist per step
+
New Cell Exploration

One-time bonus on first entry into each of the 25 grid cells. Small enough that goal-seeking remains the primary objective.

+0.5 per new cell (one-time)
+
CNN Goal Visible / Centred

Small bonus when the goal is visible. A centering bonus applies when the goal is also centred and the forward path is clear. Gating on front-sector LiDAR prevents reward for driving toward a goal blocked by a wall.

+0.5 visible; +1.5 x (1 - |offset|) when path is clear
-
Wall Collision

Hard penalty on wall contact; terminates the episode. Ensures collision avoidance is the first behaviour learned.

-200 (episode terminated)
-
Step Penalty

Small per-step cost applied every timestep. Discourages idle or inefficient movement and pushes the agent to reach the goal quickly.

-0.05 per step
-
Near-Wall Proximity

Exponential penalty when any LiDAR ray drops below 0.22 (approx. 0.66 m). Concentrated at very close range to discourage wall-hugging before contact.

-0.8 x (e^(3 x closeness) - 1)
-
Front-Wall Early Warning

Penalty when front-sector ray mean falls below 1.5 m. Gives the agent earlier warning at dead ends than the omnidirectional proximity signal alone.

-4.0 x closeness
-
Revisit Penalty

Growing per-step cost for re-entering visited cells, scaling with visit count and capped at 10x. Makes getting trapped in repeated paths progressively costly, pushing the agent toward unvisited space.

-0.05 x min(visit_count, 10) per step
-
Anti-Spin

Penalty for in-place spinning (angular speed above 1.5 rad/s). Prevents the agent from exploiting exploration bonuses through rotation alone.

-0.2 x angular_speed^2

Training Results

All training metrics were logged to Weights & Biases and TensorBoard via a custom Stable-Baselines3 callback. Results shown are from the final run: project turtlebot3-maze, run ppo-lidar-cnn-occ.

Figure 1: WandB Training Curves Left panel (rollout/ep_rew_mean): Mean episode reward per logged callback sample. The curve opens deeply negative because an untrained policy explores randomly, accumulating proximity, collision, and revisit penalties before collecting any goal reward. The reward climbs steadily, crosses zero, and stabilises above zero, showing the policy converged on goal-reaching behaviour rather than improving by chance.

Right panel (rollout/ep_len_mean): Mean episode length per callback sample. The initial rise reflects a robot that survives longer while still failing to solve the maze. The subsequent drop and stabilisation indicate the agent reaching the goal more directly rather than looping or exhausting the step budget. The combined pattern of increasing reward and decreasing episode length is characteristic of policy convergence.

X-axis note: The x-axis shows approximately 1,477 logged callback samples at fixed episode-count intervals, not raw environment steps. Rollout-buffer accounting is explained in section 6.1.

Results Discussion and Reflection

Early training behaviour (approximately 600,000 timesteps). Raw metrics appeared encouraging: success rates of 50-66% with near-zero collision counts. However, the successes did not reflect robust navigation. The robot was getting trapped in repeated circular paths through the maze. With only LiDAR and CNN in the observation, the LiDAR penalty signals dominated: they told the robot what to avoid, but not where it had already been or which areas remained unexplored. When the beacon was not visible, the agent had no spatial context to guide it toward new territory, so on difficult maps it circled through the same corridors rather than pushing forward.

A structural rather than tuning issue. The root cause was not that penalties were too weak. The observation space itself was insufficient: LiDAR provides local proximity, and CNN provides directional goal information when the beacon is visible, but neither signal tells the policy anything about the broader spatial layout it has already traversed. The early system also used seeded scenarios of varying difficulty (easy, medium, and a randomised hard case), exposing the policy to inconsistent maps while still lacking the state to distinguish useful exploration from repeated movement. The policy had learned to survive episodes without collisions, but not to solve them.

The fix: adding occupancy-style spatial memory. The key change was introducing the 25-bit visited-cell flag vector, inspired by occupancy grid mapping. This gives the policy direct knowledge of which cells it has already entered during the current episode. Combined with the new-cell exploration bonus and a growing revisit penalty (capped at 10x), the agent now has a reason to move into unvisited space rather than circling familiar corridors. The front-wall and proximity penalties (section 6.2) improved dead-end avoidance, and gating the CNN centering bonus on a front-clear LiDAR check eliminated a case where the agent drove toward a goal visible through a wall.

Final outcome. The quantitative improvements are summarised in section 7.1. More importantly, the behavioural pattern changed: the robot uses LiDAR to navigate safely, CNN goal detection when the path ahead is clear, and the visited-cell map to push into unexplored areas rather than revisiting dead ends. These signals together convert local reactive sensor readings into purposeful maze traversal.

Limitations and Future Work

The system navigates reliably within its design constraints but has several known limitations, each pointing to a concrete direction for improvement.

L1
Dependence on Reward Shaping

Each failure mode required manual identification and a new reward component. Curriculum learning over maze difficulty, intrinsic motivation methods, or sparse-reward training with imitation warm-starts could reduce this dependence and improve generalisation.

L2
No Persistent Map

The observation resets every episode. Visited-cell flags provide within-episode memory but no map that persists across runs, and LiDAR covers only the immediate 3 m neighbourhood. A recurrent policy (LSTM-PPO) or explicit topological mapping would support longer-horizon planning in larger environments.

L3
Residual Collision Rate (~2%)

Occasional contact appears to arise from assertive corridor-navigation strategies that sometimes clip walls. Tighter proximity shaping, longer training, or an inference-time safety filter could reduce this without sacrificing speed.

L4
Training Scale

Training was limited to approximately one million timesteps by available hardware and project timeline. Extended training, curriculum scheduling over maze size, a recurrent architecture, or a classical path planner as a high-level module could improve robustness beyond the 5x5 grid demonstrated here.
